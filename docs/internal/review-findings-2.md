# Independent review 2: the talk as a mixed IPP and ML jury would see it (Fri 23 Oct 2026, evening)

Gate G4 (and the numbers part of G2). Reviewer: Kritik 2, a cloud session that saw only the repository: `README.md`, `docs/`, `results/`, `presentation/pitch-script.md`, `outline.md`, the deck (`slides.pptx`, text and notes read with python-pptx, all slides rendered to images with LibreOffice) and the four `viewer-*.png` stills. There is no data here, so nothing was retrained. I did not repeat `docs/internal/review-findings.md`; items it already holds are only referred to. I edited nothing except this file and one row of `docs/PLAN.md`.

**Verdict.** The measurements are honest and mostly reproduce. The weak point is the story built on top of them: the talk sells the shipped model's accuracy, the baseline network's speed and a baseline-network demo as one object, quotes the most favourable of three accuracy protocols, and the pitch script (what the speakers actually read) disagrees with the slides on slides 4, 5 and 6. A sceptical physicist will find the 0.13 / 0.38 / 0.41 mm mix within two minutes. Fix H1 to H5 before the freeze; they are wording and one measurement, not new science.

## Ten new hostile questions (not in the pitch's ten), with truthful answers from `results/`

1. **Physicist: "Your best number is for noise-free signals. Real probes are noisy. What is your accuracy on them?"** On the organizers' noisy file the model we would use, trained with noise and drift, gives 2,38 mm on the axis, 25,8 and 38,9 mm at the limiter point and 4,65 kA for the current, on the 30 locked discharges. The clean-trained shipped model fails there (190 mm). We do not know whether the noise in the file matches the real sensors (noise is white, about 0,155 of the signal spread, `ipp1-data-checks.md`).
2. **ML: "Did you design your model while looking at the test discharges?"** The 30 locked discharges were drawn after the recipe was designed, but the design experiments used cross-validation over all 149, so these 30 were in test folds during design. Only the last choice (four recipes) was made inside the 119. Locked errors are lower than the development ones (0,133 against 0,230 mm), so quote the 0,23 mm too.
3. **ML: "Is the locked set representative?"** Not fully: 2 of 30 discharges (591 of 8 761 rows, 6,7 %) have reversed current, the bootstrap interval of the axis error is 0,099 to 0,177 mm, and on those 2 discharges the axis error is 0,454 mm, four times the rest. Too few to say anything reliable about reversed polarity.
4. **ML: "Your improved model, how much of it is real?"** Five seeds, the shipped recipe is better in 5 of 5 on all seven targets; axis, strike point and limiter gains are 42 to 60 %. The X point (R) is only 21 % better, closest seed 0,96, so we say "somewhat better". RMSE of X point R and strike point 2 is worse (0,454 against 0,305 mm and 0,638 against 0,401), and the worst single row of six targets is larger.
5. **Physicist: "Which speed do you claim, the shipped model's?"** The 0,0145 ms is the plain network. The shipped model took 0,1775 ms on a loaded machine in an earlier recipe, 12 times more, and is not yet timed on an idle machine. Both are far below the 1 to 10 ms budget; neither includes data acquisition.
6. **Physicist: "Disruptions and plasma start-up are what matters for control. How good are you there?"** Worst at the ends: last 2 % of a discharge, axis R 0,578 mm against 0,114 in the middle (shipped model, locked), Z 1,33 against 0,14 mm; the first 2 % 0,538 mm. In the cross-validation the last 2 % of rows carry 27 % of the axis error. Disruptions as such are not in the data or in our tests.
7. **ML: "A model that needs to know whether its inputs are noisy is not a model. What do you ship?"** Two models: the shipped candidate for clean inputs and the noise-trained baseline for noisy ones; there is no single winner (`ipp1-final.md`: augmented models are worse on clean data, 1,56 against 0,307 mm for the baseline). Switching by input kind is a deployment assumption we have not tested.
8. **Physicist: "Noise is independent per row and rows are 0,6 ms apart. Why not average a few samples?"** We tried three rows: with noisy inputs the axis error went from 2,64 to 2,33 mm, on clean ones it got worse (0,38 to 0,57). Longer windows were not tried; the 0,6 ms spacing is approximate (Data-Brief).
9. **Physicist: "Is a coil failure survivable?"** Not tested. The only evidence: the 16 coils alone give 0,066 summary error against 0,063 with all 24; the 8 flux loops alone 0,285; the ranking is not stable (Spearman -0,14 to 0,40 between three methods) so no single sensor is named; IPR13 survived elimination in all 5 folds.
10. **ML: "Your camera model is bad. Is the camera useless?"** It is a small network (48x64 images, 12 epochs, every 8th frame, 78 of 149 discharges) that is 1,3 to 13 times worse than the coils and gains nothing when fused (1,21 against 0,24 mm on two folds). That shows this model does not use the images well, not that the camera carries no information.
11. **Physicist: "The assignment speaks of 17 parameters, you give 16."** We predict the 16 main columns of the 31 in the file; the rest are supplementary (pitch Lead notes, point 6). We did not predict them.
12. **ML: "Out of 149 discharges, how does it do on the newest ones?"** Discharge ids appear to follow time (not confirmed with the organizers). Training on the 60 lowest and testing on the 59 highest ids: axis R 0,397 mm for the shipped model (0,216 on random folds), 1,23 mm for the baseline (0,448); smaller training set explains part of it.
13. **Business: "Why do I believe the 99,3 % limited/divertor accuracy?"** Baseline 0,993 on 5 folds, shipped candidate 0,9916 and baseline 0,9892 on the locked set; errors are timing offsets of a few rows at the transition, evenly split between both configurations (`ipp1-distribution.md`).
